SIGReg

Building a regularizer from the Epps–Pulley test that keeps a latent space from collapsing by pushing it toward an isotropic Gaussian.

Technical Discussion 2026-09-03

The tool we start from

Epps–Pulley: a Gaussian detector

Given samples \(x_1,\dots,x_N\in\mathbb{R}\), accept or reject:

\(H_0\): the data were drawn from a normal distribution
  • It never inspects a histogram directly.
  • Instead, it compares the sample's characteristic function (its Fourier fingerprint) against the Gaussian's. To be efficient, this is done over a small band of frequencies \(t\).
  • Fingerprints far apart → large statistic → reject \(H_0\).

Why this test

The characteristic function uniquely determines a distribution, so matching it checks all moments at once — not just mean and variance. It is smooth and differentiable, which is exactly what we will need for training later.

Epps & Pulley, A test for normality based on the empirical characteristic function, Biometrika, 1983.

The one object everything rests on

The characteristic function (CF)

\[ \varphi_X(t) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{\,itX}\right] = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}[\cos tX]}_{\text{real}} + i\,\underbrace{\mathbb{E}[\sin tX]}_{\text{imag}} \]
  • The Fourier transform of a distribution; it determines the distribution uniquely.
  • Standard normal target: \(\; \varphi_0(t) = e^{-t^2/2}\; \)
    purely real with \(\varphi_0(t=0) = 1\) and \(\varphi_0(t) \to 0\) as \(|t| \to \infty\).
  • From data we use the empirical CF \(\displaystyle \hat{\varphi}(t)=\tfrac1N\!\sum\nolimits_{n=1}^{N} e^{\,itx_n}\).

Right: a skewed example. Its real part (solid) sags away from \(\varphi_0\) (dashed) and its imaginary part (dotted) lifts off zero — the "fingerprint" differs.

What acceptance and rejection look like

Two samples, two verdicts

looks Gaussian

EP statistic 0.000  →  accept \(H_0\)

shifted · narrow · skewed

EP statistic 0.000  →  reject \(H_0\)

Reject \(H_0\) when \(S > c_\alpha\)   where   \(S = N\!\int_0^3\!|\hat\varphi-\varphi_0|^2 w(t)\,dt\)

The cutoff \(c_\alpha\) is not hand-picked. \(\alpha\) is the false-alarm rate you accept — here \(5\%\) of genuine Gaussians wrongly rejected. Simulate \(S\) on \(N\) true \(\mathcal{N}(0,1)\) samples and take the value only a fraction \(\alpha\) of them exceed: that \((1-\alpha)\) quantile is \(c_\alpha\). So smaller \(\alpha \Rightarrow\) larger \(c_\alpha\), stricter. The explicit \(N\) in \(S\) is chosen so that shape barely moves as \(N\) grows (\(\alpha=0.05,\ N=400 \Rightarrow c_\alpha \approx 2.9\)). A real sample still scatters to a median \(S \approx 0.76\), well under \(c_\alpha\) — that slack is sampling noise.

From a test to a training signal

SIGReg: don't test — optimize

  • In a JEPA, an encoder maps inputs to embeddings. Left unconstrained it can collapse, i.e., every input maps to (nearly) the same vector.
  • The LeJEPA paper proves the ideal embedding law is the isotropic Gaussian \(\mathcal{N}(0,I)\): it minimizes worst-case downstream risk and is, by construction, collapse-free.
  • SIGReg = the Epps–Pulley statistic used as a differentiable loss. Minimizing it pushes embeddings onto that target.

The JEPA objective

\[ \mathcal{L} = \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}}}_{\text{next-embedding}} \; + \; \lambda\, \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\text{SIGReg}}}_{\text{isotropy}} \]

No stop-gradient, no teacher/student, no whitening. SIGReg matches the entire distribution.

SIGReg introduced in LeJEPA by Balestriero & LeCun and used as the regularizer in LeWorldModel by Maes et al.

Beating the curse of dimensionality

Slice, then test in 1-D

A \(D\)-dimensional normality test is hopeless at scale. The Cramér–Wold theorem rescues us:

\[ \big(\forall\, a\in\mathbb{S}^{D-1}:\; a^{\!\top} X \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)\big) \;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\; X \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_D) \]

Matching every 1-D projection to a standard normal is the same as matching the full isotropic Gaussian.

Cramér–Wold: If \(\varphi_{a^{\!\top}X}=\varphi_{a^{\!\top}Y}\) for every \(a\in\mathbb{S}^{D-1}\), then \(X\stackrel{d}{=}Y\) — the characteristic functions of all 1-D projections determine the joint distribution.

An empirical distribution sliced along 3 directions \(a_1,a_2,a_3\). Each projection \(a_k^{\!\top}X\) has a different marginal.

From math to code

SIGReg: sketching the algorithm

  1. draw \(P\) random unit directions (the slices) \( a_p \sim \mathcal{U}(\mathbb{S}^{D-1}),\quad p = 1,\dots,P \)
  2. project the whole batch onto every slice \( Z = X A^{\top} \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times P},\quad z^{(p)} = Z_{:,p} \in \mathbb{R}^{N}\quad \text{with entries } z_n = a_p^{\top} x_n \)
  3. form each projection's empirical CF \( \hat{\varphi}_{a_p}(t) = \tfrac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N} e^{\,i\,t\,z_n} \)
  4. run the 1-D Epps–Pulley test on each slice \( \begin{aligned} D(a_p) &= N\!\int_{0}^{3} \big|\hat{\varphi}_{a_p}(t) - \varphi_0(t)\big|^{2} w(t)\,dt \\ &= N\!\int_{0}^{3}\!\Big[\big(\tfrac{1}{N}\!\sum_{n=1}^{N}\cos(t z_n)-\varphi_0(t)\big)^2 + \big(\tfrac{1}{N}\!\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sin(t z_n)\big)^2\Big]\,w(t)\,dt \end{aligned} \)
  5. average over slices \( \mathcal{L}_{\text{SIGReg}} = \mathbb{E}_{a\sim\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{S}^{D-1})}\big[D(a)\big] \approx \tfrac{1}{P}\sum\limits_{p=1}^{P} D(a_p) \)

Costs are linear in \(N\), \(D\), and \(P\); rows of \(X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times D}\) are the batch \(x_n^{\top}\), rows of \(A\in\mathbb{R}^{P\times D}\) the slices \(a_p^{\top}\); target \(\varphi_0(t) = e^{-t^2/2}\) and window \(w(t) = e^{-t^2/2}\) are numerically equal but play different roles. \(\varphi_0\) is what the empirical CF is compared against, \(w(t)\) weights that comparison across frequencies \(t\); the sums \(\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_n\cos(t z_n), \tfrac{1}{N}\sum_n\sin(t z_n)\) are the real and imaginary parts of the empirical CF. All plain tensor ops, hence autograd flows straight back into the encoder.

Takeaways

One test, one loss, no collapse

  • SIGReg = a differentiable, sliced Epps–Pulley normality test used as a loss term.
  • It drives embeddings to \(\mathcal{N}(0,I)\) — optimal and collapse-avoiding.
  • Random projections (Cramér–Wold) keep it linear in \(N\), \(P\), \(D\).
  • Its implementation is ≈50 LOC with few hyper-parameters (\(N\), \(P\), quadrature nodes).
